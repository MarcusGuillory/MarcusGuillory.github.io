This self-directed project tackles a classic challenge in the financial services industry: proactively identifying customer attrition risk. I developed a model in Python that analyzes 12 months of synthetic transaction data to detect relocation events based on shifts in spending patterns. The model uses a weighted scoring algorithm to flag at-risk customers, culminating in a data-driven recommendation for a proactive retention strategy.

Identifying Customer Archetypes

  • Developed a weighted scoring algorithm in Python to quantify relocation signals from raw transaction data.

  • Visualized the score over time to identify and define three key customer archetypes: clear movers, true non-movers, and anomalous "super commuters."

  • Established a score threshold of 0.7, demonstrating an understanding of how to distinguish a significant behavioral shift from normal day-to-day spending "noise."

Model Performance & Validation

  • Applied a business rule (score > 0.7 for 3+ consecutive months) to classify all 250 customers as either a "Likely Mover" or "Not a Mover."

  • Achieved 100% Recall, proving the model's effectiveness as a "safety net" that successfully identifies every true mover and ensures no retention opportunity is missed.

  • Interpreted the 37% Precision as a strategic trade-off, demonstrating an understanding of how to balance model performance with business costs and objectives.

Strategic Insight:
Segmenting Attrition Risk

  • Analyzed the demographic composition of the population flagged as "Likely Movers" to uncover the key business insight.

  • Discovered that the 'Affluent' customer segment represents the largest and most valuable group at high risk of attrition due to relocation.

  • This key finding directly informed the final, data-driven recommendation for a targeted retention program aimed at high-value customers.

Methodology: Synthetic
Data Generation

  • Created a custom Python script to generate a robust, multi-table synthetic dataset, simulating 12 months of banking activity for 250 customers.

  • Utilized the Pandas, NumPy, and Faker libraries to produce 8 interconnected CSV files representing customers, accounts, and transactions.

  • Implemented a random seed to ensure the dataset is 100% reproducible, a key best practice for verifiable and reliable data analysis.
